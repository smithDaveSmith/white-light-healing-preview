Your first visit

Your first reflexology
treatment.

A simple guide to arriving, settling in and deciding whether a session feels right for you.

Original article: 29 May 2026 · Concept revision: 15 October 2026

EDITORIAL & EVIDENCE CORRECTION

This guide replaces the original explanations about detoxification, energy blockages and symptoms proving stronger immunity. Those mechanisms are not established. NCCIH’s reflexology overview explains the evidence limitations ↗

Start with a conversation.

Your first appointment is a chance to tell Caroline what brings you here. Discuss comfort, pressure, any relevant health conditions, pregnancy and clinical advice you have received. Ask questions before treatment begins.

Get comfortable.

Foot reflexology involves pressure applied to the feet using fingers and thumbs. You remove your shoes and socks for this part of a session. Caroline’s practice uses a reclining zero-gravity chair, as described on her website.

You remain in control of the experience. Say if you would like less pressure, a different position, a break or to stop.

Keep your expectations grounded.

A reflexology session is complementary wellbeing care. Claims that particular foot points treat the corresponding organs have not been proven. A session is not a diagnosis, a fertility treatment or a replacement for prescribed care.

After the appointment.

Allow yourself time afterwards if you would find that helpful. Drink according to your normal needs. There is no established need to “flush out toxins” after reflexology.

Unexpected or persistent symptoms should not be treated as proof that a therapy is working. Contact Caroline with session-related concerns and seek appropriate medical advice for health symptoms.

You decide what happens next.

There is no required course length in this guide. Talk about whether another session would be useful to you, the available options and the cost before booking.
